Sensible heat flux over Arctic sea ice from airborne eddy covariance and bulk estimates

Leipzig Institute for Meteorology (LIM), Leipzig University

This site is the electronic supplement to Evaluation of reanalysis and satellite products of sensible heat flux over Arctic sea ice against airborne eddy-covariance measurements by J. J. Müller, I. Staudinger, F. Gebhardt, D. J. Simon, L. Köhler, J. Hartmann, A. Ehrlich, M. Schäfer, M. D. Shupe and M. Wendisch (submitted to Atmospheric Measurement Techniques). It documents the method, the code that produces every number, table and figure of the paper, and the data from which they are derived.

During the BACSAM-II campaign (Svalbard, April 2024), the T-Bird was towed 20–30 m above the sea ice and measured wind and temperature at 100 Hz. The sensible heat flux derived from these data by eddy covariance is the reference for bulk estimates driven by in-situ, satellite and reanalysis inputs.

How to use this supplement¶

To check the paper. Paper and code locates each section, equation, table and figure of the paper in the code, and Figures shows each figure with its caption and the script that draws it. pixi run check recomputes every number printed in the paper from the data in the repository and compares it with the committed reference; pixi run all also rebuilds the tables and figures (Reproduction). Neither requires programming knowledge.

To reuse code or data. The analysis package fluxpy (eddy covariance, bulk transfer, stability schemes, uncertainty) is documented in the API reference; the data files and their variables are described under Data files. The code is released under the MIT License; the subsets of third-party products remain under the licences of their providers. Please cite the paper and the archived version of the repository (Müller (2026), CITATION.cff).

To follow the method. The Method chapters derive the eddy-covariance and the bulk flux of a single segment by hand and recompute the central results of the paper; the Glossary defines the symbols. No prior knowledge of the code is assumed.

Flux estimates¶

Six estimates of the same flux are compared on the same 91 flight segments of 5 km. From top to bottom, measured inputs are successively replaced by products with pan-Arctic coverage, so that a loss of skill can be attributed to the replaced input.

Contents¶

Five chapters that recompute the central numbers of the paper: the eddy-covariance and bulk flux of one segment, the comparison, and sensitivity and uncertainty.

Figures

The eight figures of the paper with their captions, their construction and the scripts that produce them.

Reproduction

pixi run all recomputes every number, table and figure and compares the numbers with a committed reference.

Paper and code

The location of each section, equation, table and figure of the paper in the code, and an annotated copy of the manuscript.

API reference

Every module, constant, class and function of the analysis package fluxpy, and every script, generated from the source code.

Main results¶

  1. The surface temperature determines whether a bulk estimate follows the observed variability. Driven by ERA5 alone, the bulk flux recovers only a small part of the observed amplitude. Replacing the ERA5 surface temperature by the MODIS retrieval improves amplitude and correlation, mainly on segments with a heterogeneous surface. → Chapter 4

  2. With measured inputs, the conventional transfer coefficient is too large. The in-situ bulk estimate has the highest correlation but overestimates the amplitude. The scalar roughness ratio αT rescales the amplitude without changing the correlation. → Chapter 5

  3. The choice of stability scheme is of minor importance under the near-neutral spring conditions sampled. → Chapter 3
