3 · Bulk flux estimates

Leipzig Institute for Meteorology (LIM), Leipzig University

Without turbulence measurements, the flux is estimated from mean quantities:

HS=ρ cp Ch U (θs−θair)H_\mathrm{S} = \rho\, c_p\, C_\mathrm{h}\, U\, (\theta_\mathrm{s} - \theta_\mathrm{air})HS​=ρcp​Ch​U(θs​−θair​)
(1)

the wind speed UUU times the surface–air temperature difference, scaled by the transfer coefficient ChC_\mathrm{h}Ch​, which describes the efficiency of turbulent mixing. ChC_\mathrm{h}Ch​ depends on

  • the roughness of the surface: z0,mz_{0,m}z0,m​ for momentum and z0,T=αT z0,mz_{0,T} = \alpha_T\, z_{0,m}z0,T​=αT​z0,m​ for heat;

  • the stability, expressed by the bulk Richardson number RibRi_\mathrm{b}Rib​: stable stratification (Rib>0Ri_\mathrm{b} > 0Rib​>0, surface colder than the air) suppresses turbulence, unstable stratification enhances it.

This chapter computes HS,obs,bulkH_\mathrm{S,obs,bulk}HS,obs,bulk​ for one segment and shows the dependence of ChC_\mathrm{h}Ch​ on stability and on αT\alpha_TαT​.

Note

Paper: Sect. 3.2, Eq. (2), Appendix B, Table B1. Code: fluxpy/bulk.py (steps B1–B3), fluxpy/stability.py (Eq. B3), fluxpy/stability_lib.py (the eight stability schemes), fluxpy/util.py (constants, potential temperature, pressures).

Temperature difference and wind speed¶

The surface temperature is taken from the KT-19 infrared pyrometer, air temperature and wind from the T-Bird, all averaged over the 5 km segment. Both temperatures are converted to potential temperature with the pressure at their own height. Pressure is measured at the T-Bird, about 23 m above the ice, not at the surface; the surface pressure follows from the hypsometric equation,

ps=pzexp⁡ ⁣(g zRd Tˉ),p_\mathrm{s} = p_z \exp\!\left(\frac{g\,z}{R_d\,\bar T}\right),ps​=pz​exp(Rd​Tˉgz​),
(2)

about 3 hPa above the T-Bird value. Using pzp_zpz​ at the surface would make θs\theta_\mathrm{s}θs​ too warm by gz/cp≈0.23g z / c_p \approx 0.23gz/cp​≈0.23 K, which is not negligible against typical temperature differences of one to two kelvin. The reanalysis-driven estimates apply the same rule: the ERA5 surface pressure for θs\theta_\mathrm{s}θs​, and the pressure of model level 136 (about 31 m; fluxpy.util.era5_level_pressure) for the air temperature of that level.

from fluxpy.util import surface_pressure_hypsometric
T_s, T_a, p, z = float(s.t_kt19), float(s.T_mean), float(s.p_mean), float(s.altitude)
u, v = float(s.u_mean), float(s.v_mean); U = np.hypot(u, v)
p_s = float(surface_pressure_hypsometric(p, z, T_mean=0.5 * (T_s + T_a)))
th_s, th_a = float(potential_temperature(T_s, p_s)), float(potential_temperature(T_a, p))

      f"(with the T-Bird pressure at the surface it would be {float(potential_temperature(T_s, p)) - th_a:+.2f} K);  wind {U:.1f} m/s")

Bulk Richardson number¶

Rib=gθˉ Δθ ΔzΔu2+Δv2Ri_\mathrm{b} = \frac{g}{\bar\theta}\,\frac{\Delta\theta\,\Delta z}{\Delta u^2 + \Delta v^2}Rib​=θˉg​Δu2+Δv2ΔθΔz​
(3)

between the surface (zero wind) and the measurement height; negative values indicate unstable stratification.

Ri = float(bulk_richardson_2pt(dtheta=th_a - th_s, dz=z, du=u, dv=v, theta_ref=0.5 * (th_s + th_a), eps_wind2=cfg.ri_eps_wind2))
print(f"Ri_b = {Ri:+.2f}  ->", "unstable (surface warmer than air)" if Ri < 0 else "stable")
Ri_b = -0.89  -> unstable (surface warmer than air)

Roughness lengths¶

Within 5 km the surface consists of open water, thin ice and snow-covered ice. Each KT-19 sample is classified by its temperature (above −1.8 °C: open water; at or below −20 °C: snow-covered ice; in between: thin ice). The effective roughness length is the logarithmic average over the three fractions.

fr = np.array([float(s.frac_open), float(s.frac_thin_ice), float(s.frac_thick_ice)])
z0 = float(np.exp(fr @ np.log([cfg.z0_ocean, cfg.z0_thin_ice, cfg.z0_thick_ice]))); zT = cfg.zt_ratio * z0
print(f"fractions open / thin / snow-covered: {fr.round(2)}  ->  z0m = {z0:.1e} m,  z0T = alpha_T * z0m = {zT:.1e} m")
fractions open / thin / snow-covered: [0. 1. 0.]  ->  z0m = 1.0e-04 m,  z0T = alpha_T * z0m = 1.0e-05 m

Dependence of ChC_\mathrm{h}Ch​ on stability¶

The stability dependence follows from Monin–Obukhov similarity theory, for which several formulations exist. The paper uses Gryanik et al. (2020, GLGS20) as reference and tests seven alternatives. The shading marks the range of RibRi_\mathrm{b}Rib​ sampled during the campaign (central 90 % of the segments). All schemes share the unstable branch; on the stable branch they differ, but there ChC_\mathrm{h}Ch​ and the flux are small in every scheme. The effect on the comparison is quantified in Chapter 5.

lo, hi = np.nanpercentile(FIG.Ri_obs.values, [5, 95])                 # central 90 % of the sampled Ri_b
ri = np.linspace(min(-1.0, lo), max(0.5, hi), 300)
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(6.5, 3.4))
for name in ["GLGS20", "BD", "HB88", "BH91", "CB05", "GAFGP07"]:

ax.axvspan(lo, hi, color="0.85")
ax.axvline(0, color="k", lw=0.6); ax.set_xlabel("bulk Richardson number"); ax.set_ylabel("$C_h$ × 10$^3$"); ax.legend(ncol=2, fontsize=8)

Dependence of ChC_\mathrm{h}Ch​ on αT\alpha_TαT​¶

The scalar roughness ratio αT=z0,T/z0,m\alpha_T = z_{0,T}/z_{0,m}αT​=z0,T​/z0,m​ is poorly constrained over sea ice: models use values between 1 (ERA5) and 0.1 (large-eddy simulations; the reference of the paper), whereas theory and earlier aircraft measurements indicate much smaller values. ChC_\mathrm{h}Ch​ depends on it logarithmically:

a = np.logspace(-7, 0, 50)
ch = np.array([Cd_Ch_noniterative(0.0, 24.0, 3e-4, 3e-4 * x, "GLGS20")[1] for x in a])
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(6, 3)); ax.semilogx(a, ch / ch[np.argmin(abs(a - 0.1))], color="k")
ax.axvline(0.1, color="C2", ls="--"); ax.axvline(1e-2, color="C1", ls="--")
ax.set_xlabel(r"$\alpha_T$"); ax.set_ylabel(r"$C_h$ relative to $\alpha_T$ = 0.1")
print(f"alpha_T from 1e-1 to 1e-4 (three orders of magnitude) changes C_h by a factor {ch[np.argmin(abs(a - 1e-4))] / ch[np.argmin(abs(a - 0.1))]:.2f}")
alpha_T from 1e-1 to 1e-4 (three orders of magnitude) changes C_h by a factor 0.66

Summary. The bulk flux is the product of wind speed, temperature difference and ChC_\mathrm{h}Ch​, which contains roughness and stability. Roughness scales the flux and thus changes its amplitude, but not where along the track the flux is large or small; that is determined by the temperature difference and the wind. This distinction structures the results of Chapters 4 and 5.
