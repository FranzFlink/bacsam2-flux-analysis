5 · Sensitivity and uncertainty

Leipzig Institute for Meteorology (LIM), Leipzig University

With measured inputs, the bulk method overestimates the flux amplitude (Chapter 4). To identify the cause, the paper varies one choice within the transfer coefficient at a time (scalar roughness ratio αT\alpha_TαT​, stability scheme, momentum roughness) and perturbs the two inputs, temperature difference and wind speed.

Note

Paper: Sect. 4.2, Table 4, Fig. 7, Appendix C (Table C1), Table 2. Code: fluxpy/sensitivity.py (the configurations), scripts/calc_bulk_and_ec_fluxes.py (stages 3 and 4), fluxpy/uncertainty.py (Table 2), scripts/make_manuscript_tables.py.

Figure 5.1. Slope bbb (left) and correlation rrr (right) of the bulk estimates with measured inputs (green) and with MODIS + ERA5 inputs (orange) as functions of αT\alpha_TαT​; the dashed line marks the ideal slope of 1. The table lists the same values.

With measured inputs, the amplitude is matched (slope 1) near αT=10−2\alpha_T = 10^{-2}αT​=10−2, the amplitude-matched configuration of the paper, one order of magnitude below the conventional value. The correlation is unaffected, since roughness only rescales the flux (Chapter 3).

This is not interpreted as a measurement of z0,Tz_{0,T}z0,T​: any error that scales the flux, for instance in the surface temperature, is absorbed by the same parameter. The value of αT\alpha_TαT​ that gives the in-situ estimate the correct amplitude further reduces the amplitude of the ERA5–MODIS estimate, which is already too small; a single αT\alpha_TαT​ cannot correct both.

No choice within the transfer coefficient changes the correlation much. With the measured inputs, only the roughness lengths change the amplitude appreciably, and the eight stability schemes are practically indistinguishable: they share the functions for unstable stratification and differ only under stable stratification, where the flux is small (Chapter 3). With the MODIS and ERA5 inputs, which are stably stratified on more segments, the stability schemes change the amplitude about as much as the roughness choices do.

The table gives the change of the bulk flux per unit change of one input: the median over the segments and, in brackets, the 25th and 75th percentiles. A change of 1 K in the temperature difference has a much larger effect than 1 m s⁻¹ in wind speed, and the typical air–surface temperature difference of one to two kelvin is comparable to the uncertainty of a satellite surface temperature. The surface temperature product is therefore decisive (Chapter 4).

Summary. The measured reference is known to better than 1 W m⁻². A bulk estimate driven by satellite and reanalysis inputs is uncertain by several W m⁻², more than the typical flux; surface temperature, air temperature and the large ERA5 wind uncertainty contribute comparably. Whether an estimate follows the observed variability is determined by the surface temperature (Chapter 4), its amplitude by the transfer coefficient; improving pan-Arctic flux estimates requires both.
