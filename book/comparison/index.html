Skill metrics¶

Most measured fluxes lie within a few W m⁻² of zero (Chapter 1), so an estimate close to zero everywhere has a small error without containing information. The table therefore includes the trivial estimate H=0H = 0H=0 on every segment; its mean absolute error (MAE) and RMSE can be compared with those of the actual estimates.

The paper uses two separate measures:

  • slope bbb of the regression of the estimate on the EC flux: the fraction of the observed amplitude recovered (ideal: 1);

  • correlation rrr: agreement of estimate and reference in their variability (ideal: 1).

The slope equals rrr times the ratio of the standard deviations of estimate and reference. A slope near 1 can therefore result from the correct amplitude with good correlation or from excess variance with poor correlation; bbb and rrr are interpreted together.

Note

Paper: Sect. 4.1, Table 3, Figs. 4–6. Code: fluxpy/sensitivity.py (skill_metrics), scripts/manuscript_numbers.py (every number of Sect. 4.1), figures_manuscript/fig06_flux_scatter.py.

LEG = np.cumsum(np.r_[0, np.diff(T.values) / np.timedelta64(1, "m")] > 10)          # a new leg after a gap of > 10 min

def r_leg(h):
    """correlation after removing the mean of every leg: only the variability along the legs counts"""
    d = pd.DataFrame({"ec": EC, "h": h, "leg": LEG}).dropna()
    a = d.groupby("leg")[["ec", "h"]].transform(lambda s: s - s.mean())
    return np.corrcoef(a.ec, a.h)[0, 1]

rows = {}
for key, name in NAMES.items():
    h = FIG[key].values; ok = np.isfinite(h)
    m = skill_metrics(EC, h, key)
    rows[name] = {"slope b": m["slope"], "r": m["r"], "r_leg": r_leg(h), "sd ratio": m["std_ratio"], "bias (W m-2)": m["bias"],

(CARRA is not available on 13 April, hence the smaller n.) The zero estimate has a smaller MAE and RMSE than every estimate in the table except the one with measured inputs: in this weak-flux regime, these error measures reward conservative estimates, not skill.

rlegr_\mathrm{leg}rleg​ is the correlation after removing the mean of each leg from both series (Table 3 of the paper). It measures whether an estimate follows the flux from segment to segment along a leg, rather than only the differences between flight days. Only the estimate with measured inputs does so clearly.

The model-diagnosed fluxes of ERA5 and CARRA overestimate the amplitude (slope above 1) and are biased low. They follow the observed variability better than the offline ERA5-only estimate, which the paper attributes to their internal skin temperature and much larger scalar roughness (Sect. 2.2 and Discussion), but follow it only weakly along the legs.

Figure 4.1. The three offline bulk estimates against the EC flux, one point per segment, with the 1:1 line (dashed) and the regression line (solid). Left to right: measured inputs, MODIS surface temperature with the ERA5 atmosphere, ERA5 only. Figure 6 of the paper shows the same comparison together with the model-diagnosed fluxes.

From left to right, the panels show the main result of the paper (numbers in the panel titles):

  • Measured inputs: the highest correlation, but the amplitude is overestimated (slope above 1). With measured inputs, the conventional transfer coefficient is too large (Chapter 5).

  • MODIS surface temperature: with the ERA5 atmosphere unchanged, amplitude and correlation both increase.

  • ERA5 only: the regression line is nearly flat; a small part of the amplitude is recovered and the correlation is weak.

Effect of the satellite surface temperature¶

If the benefit of MODIS arises from resolving leads and thin ice that ERA5 does not represent, it should be greatest for segments with a heterogeneous surface. The segments are split at the median of the within-segment standard deviation of the KT-19 surface temperature.

sd = SEG.T_sfc_std.values; rs, era = FIG.H_rs_bulk.values, FIG.H_era_bulk.values
ok = np.isfinite(EC) & np.isfinite(rs) & np.isfinite(era) & np.isfinite(sd); med = np.median(sd[ok])
rows = {}
for label, m in (("heterogeneous half", ok & (sd > med)), ("homogeneous half", ok & (sd <= med))):
    rows[label] = {"n": int(m.sum()), "r  ERA5 only": np.corrcoef(EC[m], era[m])[0, 1], "r  with MODIS": np.corrcoef(EC[m], rs[m])[0, 1],
                   "slope  ERA5 only": np.polyfit(EC[m], era[m], 1)[0], "slope  with MODIS": np.polyfit(EC[m], rs[m], 1)[0]}
print(f"median within-segment standard deviation of the surface temperature: {med:.1f} K"); pd.DataFrame(rows).T.round(2)
