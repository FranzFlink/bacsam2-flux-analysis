2 · Eddy-covariance reference flux

Leipzig Institute for Meteorology (LIM), Leipzig University

Turbulent eddies transport heat upward when rising air (w′>0w' > 0w′>0) is on average warmer (θ′>0\theta' > 0θ′>0) than sinking air. The sensible heat flux is this covariance multiplied by the heat capacity of air:

HS=ρ cp w′θ′‾H_\mathrm{S} = \rho\, c_p\, \overline{w'\theta'}HS​=ρcp​w′θ′
(1)

This chapter derives the flux of one 5 km segment from the 100 Hz data and reproduces the value stored for it in the segment file data/observations/ar/analysis_ready_polar6_turbulent_fluxes_latest_5km.nc. That file holds one row per 5 km segment (EC flux, random error, integrity check and segment means) and is written by scripts/run_ec_aggregation.py; its variables are listed under Data files.

Note

Paper: Sect. 3.1, Eq. (1), Fig. 3, Appendix A. Code: fluxpy/eddy_covariance.py (band_covariance, band_covariance_random_error, segment_flux), fluxpy/qc.py (integrity check), scripts/ec_window_length.py (Fig. 3), scripts/ec_quality.py (every number of Appendix A).

Detrended time series¶

Vertical wind www and potential temperature θ\thetaθ are measured at 100 Hz. The platform motion has been removed from the wind with the inertial navigation data; the potential rather than the virtual potential temperature is used because the air is cold and dry (paper Sect. 3.1). Removing a linear trend from both series yields the fluctuations w′w'w′ and θ′\theta'θ′.

from scipy.signal import detrend
w, th = detrend(seg.w.values), detrend(seg.theta.values)
x_km = np.arange(w.size) / 100 * float(seg.tas.mean()) / 1000
fig, axs = plt.subplots(3, 1, figsize=(8, 5), sharex=True)
axs[0].plot(x_km, w, lw=0.4, color="C0"); axs[0].set_ylabel("w' (m s$^{-1}$)")
axs[1].plot(x_km, th, lw=0.4, color="C3"); axs[1].set_ylabel(r"$\theta$' (K)")
axs[2].plot(x_km, np.cumsum(w * th) / w.size, color="k"); axs[2].set_ylabel(r"running sum of w'$\theta$'"); axs[2].set_xlabel("distance along the segment (km)")
print(f"plain covariance of the detrended series: {np.mean(w * th):.4f} K m/s")

Cospectrum and flux band¶

The cospectrum distributes the covariance over frequency. At the mean airspeed of 62 m s⁻¹, a frequency fff corresponds to an eddy length of 62 m s−1/f62\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}/f62ms−1/f. The flux is the cospectrum integrated between 0.1 and 20 Hz (eddies of about 3 to 620 m): slower variations are attributed to mesoscale changes along the track, faster ones to instrument noise. Only the shaded band enters the flux.

n, fs = w.size, 100.0
f = np.fft.rfftfreq(n, 1 / fs)
co = 2 * np.real(np.fft.rfft(w) * np.conj(np.fft.rfft(th))) / n**2       # one-sided cospectrum; sums to the covariance
edges = np.logspace(np.log10(f[1]), np.log10(50), 40); mid = np.sqrt(edges[:-1] * edges[1:])
binned = np.array([co[(f >= a) & (f < b)].sum() for a, b in zip(edges[:-1], edges[1:])])

fig, axs = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(9, 3.2))
axs[0].bar(mid, binned, width=np.diff(edges), color="0.4"); axs[0].set_xscale("log")
axs[0].set_ylabel("covariance per frequency bin (K m s$^{-1}$)"); axs[0].set_title("cospectrum")
axs[1].plot(f[1:], np.cumsum(co[1:][::-1])[::-1], color="k"); axs[1].set_xscale("log")
axs[1].set_ylabel("covariance from f upwards (K m s$^{-1}$)"); axs[1].set_title("cumulative from high to low frequency")
for ax in axs:
    ax.axvspan(0.1, 20, color="C1", alpha=0.15); ax.set_xlabel("frequency (Hz)")
in_band = band_covariance(w, th, fs, 0.1, 20.0)
print(f"covariance inside 0.1-20 Hz: {in_band:.4f} K m/s = {in_band / np.mean(w * th) * 100:.0f} % of the total covariance of this segment")

Integrity check and random error (Appendix A)¶

Two properties are assessed separately.

Integrity. Resolved turbulence shows the inertial-subrange slope in the power spectrum of www, ∣sw+5/3∣≤0.4|s_w + 5/3| \le 0.4∣sw​+5/3∣≤0.4. The slope is reported for every segment; no segment is excluded by it. Table A1 of the paper repeats the comparison on the segments within tolerances of 0.3 to 0.6.

Precision. A covariance from about 81 s of data carries a random error, because only a finite number of eddies is sampled. It is estimated from the data (Finkelstein and Sims, 2001). For a true flux of zero, the estimate exceeds 1, 2 and 3 σrand\sigma_\mathrm{rand}σrand​ by chance in 32 %, 5 % and 0.3 % of cases.

Fluxes that do not exceed their random error are retained. Over compact sea ice a flux indistinguishable from zero is the typical and physically valid result; discarding such segments would bias the reference towards large fluxes. For the same reason, criteria formulated as ratios to the flux (such as signal-to-noise ratios) are not applied: they reject small fluxes and accept large ones irrespective of the quality of the measurement.

from fluxpy.qc import segment_spectra, turbulence_resolved
from fluxpy.eddy_covariance import band_covariance_random_error
slope = segment_spectra(w=seg.w.values, theta=seg.theta.values, u=seg.u.values, v=seg.v.values, fs=100.0)["slope_w"]
sigma = rho * CP * band_covariance_random_error(w, th, fs)
print(f"this segment: spectral slope of w = {slope:.2f} -> turbulence resolved: {turbulence_resolved(slope)}")

Segment length (Fig. 3)¶

Short windows under-sample the larger eddies; long windows average over leads and ice. Each of the six low-level legs is cut into consecutive sub-windows of different length, and their mean flux is compared with the flux of the stretch they cover, i.e. the leg without the remainder shorter than one window. Where a window edge cuts through a strong flux event, the ratio depends on where the grid of windows starts; it is therefore averaged over five grid positions, shifted by a fifth of the window length each (scripts/ec_window_length.py).

wl = pd.read_csv(AR / "ec_leg_length_sensitivity/ec_sensitivity_summary.csv").query("length_km >= 1 and label != 'full leg'")

Uncertainty of the EC flux¶

The total uncertainty combines, in quadrature, the random error (above) and the instrument error (5 %, from the flow calibration of the five-hole probe). The placement spread, obtained by shifting the 5 km window along the leg in 0.05 km steps (scripts/ec_segment_placement.py), is an independent, empirical estimate of the random error. It is reported as a cross-check and not added.

from fluxpy.uncertainty import uncertainty_budget
U = uncertainty_budget(FIG)
pd.DataFrame({k.replace("ec_", ""): [np.nanmedian(U[k]), np.nanpercentile(U[k], 25), np.nanpercentile(U[k], 75)]
              for k in ("ec_random", "ec_instrument", "ec_total", "ec_placement")},

Appendix A of the paper contains two further checks, printed by pixi run ec-quality: the comparison is insensitive to the screening of the fluxes (Table A1, eight alternative screenings), and the pitch resonance of the towed T-Bird near 1 Hz reduces the larger fluxes by less than a tenth (median), with practically no effect on the comparison.

Summary. The reference flux is a band-limited covariance that can be recomputed from the 100 Hz data in a few lines. Its uncertainty is dominated by the finite number of eddies in 5 km, as confirmed by the placement spread, not by the instrument.
